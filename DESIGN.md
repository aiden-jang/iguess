# iguess: draw badly, guess wildly

Open studio uses ink typography, acid-green accents, rough little drawings, and dry commentary.
Keep the controls calm and the game quick.

## Visual language

- Paper #f2f2e9, ink #202522, acid #d8ed79, muted green-gray and a small warm pink accent.
- Inter carries confident, tightly spaced headlines and all functional text. Fredoka is for
  the wordmark, annotations and hand-drawn personality rather than every heading.
- Use open composition, rules, and one strong play surface. Avoid wrapping every region in
  the same rounded panel. The entry form is a contrasting ink surface with an acid action.
- The drawing stays white in both themes. Gallery prints stay paper-colored in dark mode.
- Selection is labeled and outlined. Never change or joke-rewrite a player’s own content.
- Animate useful feedback and small expressions locally; respect reduced motion and the browser-local motion preference. Celebrations are
  restrained and earned, with no emoji rain after a zero-score game.

## Product priorities

Enter: a name, a mode, and one primary action. Put room codes and extra modes behind a labeled
expansion. Invitations identify their room and defer the live mode to its host. Avatar selection
is available without becoming a required step. Public help, privacy and terms remain reachable.

Set up: show the host’s rules and start controls in the lobby. A wide room also shows who is
present and ready above room chat. Players, scores and personal feedback preferences are in
the header disclosure at every width.

Play: name the drawer or show the drawer’s word in the turn heading. Keep round details, the hint
and the timer near the canvas. Place the composer below the canvas on a phone and beside it at
larger sizes. The conversation stays visible beneath the composer, and drawing tools appear
when the player can use them.

Finish: place Play again and result sharing next to the outcome when space allows. Show numbered
prints in two columns where they fit and one on small phones. Identify the artists, keep replay and
export available, and ground awards in game events. Handle empty, tied and zero-score games honestly.

## Voice

Nonchalant, funny, specific. Let the situation and the machine’s confidence supply the joke.
Use dry asides and short reactions; avoid slang substitutions, fake urgency and constant
exclamation marks. Instructions, errors, confirmations and privacy text stay clear.

## Acceptance

Inspect desktop and phone layouts in light and dark palettes, including 320 px and landscape.
Check keyboard joining, disclosure controls, dialogs, drawing, guessing, reconnects, results,
replay and sharing. Use disposable local rooms and mock AI. Inspect temporary captures and record limitations in the change description. Browser checks do not establish physical-device or paid-AI
quality.

The lobby has a small pencil-and-paper sketch, and guests waiting for a word choice see a blank-paper/hourglass doodle. Both are decorative, static vector art in the studio palette; they never depend on the secret word, player drawings or scores. The actual drawing surface and result gallery stay primary.

Custom-word validation says when a draft has not been applied. Enter saves without blurring the field; guest settings explain host ownership rather than offering unavailable editing instructions.

Drawing Relay uses the same studio chrome for a private prompt, a white board and one pass
action. Drawers pass from beside the canvas on wide screens. On phones the pass action or
guess composer follows the canvas. The end gallery pairs each drawing with the next guess
and shows missed passes and replacement words in sequence.
